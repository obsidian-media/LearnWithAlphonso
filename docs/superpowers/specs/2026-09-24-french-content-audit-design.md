## 1. Problem

French shipped 500 lessons in a single push (2026-09-21, 125 → 500) and
has never had a content-quality pass. `docs/BACKLOG.md` has carried
"Native-speaker review pass on the French content bank" as an open item
since the module shipped, and the 2026-09-22 automated consistency scan
covered French only for _structural_ defects, not quality.

Meanwhile English has had three rounds of work (correctness audit,
listening, speaking). The two courses are no longer comparable, and the
documented claim that all three courses are at "full parity" is now
false in two separate ways — see section 4.

---

## 2. Why French is not English — read this first

Three things the English work relied on do not exist for French.

### 2.1 The distractor fix cannot be copied

The English overhaul's headline fix was distractor quality: cloze packs
pooled every answer regardless of word class, so verb slots were offered
nouns. It was fixed by ranking candidates on part-of-speech affinity
(`src/lib/distractor-affinity.ts`), driven by `src/data/answer-pos.ts`.

`answer-pos.ts` is **generated by `compromise`, an English-only NLP
library** (`scripts/gen-answer-pos.ts`). There is no French equivalent
wired into this repo.

Verified 2026-09-24 — imports of `distractor-affinity`:

| File                                | Imports |
| ----------------------------------- | ------- |
| `src/data/lesson-bank.ts` (English) | 1       |
| `src/data/lesson-bank-fr.ts`        | **0**   |
| `src/data/lesson-bank-es.ts`        | **0**   |

**So the exact bug that motivated the entire English overhaul is still
fully present in French, and no amount of copying fixes it.**

### 2.2 The fix lives in a duplicated generator, not the shared engine

This is the part that is easy to get wrong. There are **two** pack
generators in this repo:

- `src/data/lesson-bank.ts` (English) has its **own** `pickDistractors`
  and its own expansion logic. Its own comment says it "predates the
  shared engine and hasn't been consolidated onto it". The affinity
  ranking was added _there_.
- `src/data/bank-engine.ts` is the shared engine. `lesson-bank-fr.ts`
  and `lesson-bank-es.ts` both call its `unitsFromBank`. Its
  `pickDistractors` has **no ranking at all**.

Two consequences:

1. Adding French ranking inside `lesson-bank-fr.ts`, mirroring English,
   would deepen the duplication the backlog already wants removed.
2. Adding ranking to `bank-engine.ts` instead fixes **French and Spanish
   at once** — a strictly better place for it, and the recommended path.
   It does not require consolidating English first.

### 2.3 French has no hand-written content to fall back on

English has 9 hand-written units (171 questions). The English audit
checked them and found them **clean** — "the defect is specific to
generated pack content", with notably strong hand-authored distractors.

French has none:

```ts
// src/data/curriculum-fr.ts — the entire content definition
export const curriculumFr: Unit[] = generatedUnitsFr();
```

`curriculum-fr.ts` is 39 lines and contains no questions at all — only
index and lookup helpers. **100% of French content is generated**, so
100% of it is exposed to whatever the generator does wrong. There is no
known-good subset to compare against, and no clean in-course baseline
for calibrating what "good" looks like here.

## 3. Where French content actually lives

| Path                                                        | Lines | Role                                                 |
| ----------------------------------------------------------- | ----- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| `src/data/lesson-bank-fr.ts`                                | 3,293 | **All** French content. 100 packs.                   |
| `src/data/curriculum-fr.ts`                                 | 39    | Expansion + index only. No content.                  |
| `src/data/placement-fr.ts`                                  | 367   | Placement pool, 45 questions. Separate from lessons. |
| `src/data/bank-engine.ts`                                   | —     | Shared generator. Owns `pickDistractors` for FR/ES.  |
| `ios/LearnWithAlphonso/Resources/curriculum-fr.json`        | —     | Generated, committed.                                |
| `ios/LearnWithAlphonsoKit/.../Resources/curriculum-fr.json` | —     | Second committed copy.                               |

Both iOS copies are regenerated by `bun scripts/export-ios-content.ts`
and **CI fails if they are stale** (added in PR #87). Never hand-edit
them.

A pack is a terse block; the generator expands 25 lines into 5-question
lessons. Two kinds exist in French:

Cloze — the answer is a conjugated form, distractors pooled from the
same pack:

```
kind: "cloze"
note: Present-tense "je" conjugation of common verbs.
data:
  Je ___ (parler) français.|parle
  Je ___ (manger) une pomme.|mange
  Je ___ (avoir) un chat.|ai
```

Pair — English prompt, French answer, distractors pooled from the same
pack:

```
kind: "pair"
prompt: How do you say "%s" in French?
data:
  hello (informal)|Salut
  good evening|Bonsoir
```

## 5. The three quality axes

Keep these separate. Conflating them is how this work becomes
unfinishable.

| Axis                       | What it catches                                                                          | Can an agent do it?                                   |
| -------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| **Structural**             | duplicate ids, out-of-range answers, empty strings, malformed lines, id-regex violations | **Yes** — already partly automated                    |
| **Distractor quality**     | grammatically impossible or semantically absurd wrong answers                            | **Yes, but blocked** — needs a French POS source (§8) |
| **Linguistic correctness** | grammar, naturalness, idiom, register                                                    | **No** — needs a native speaker                       |

**Phase 1 of this handoff is the structural axis only.** Distractor
quality is section 8 and depends on a decision that has not been made.
Linguistic correctness is explicitly out of scope for any agent session
and stays on the backlog until a human is commissioned.

## 6. Phase 1 — structural audit

### 6.1 Take an id baseline first

**Before touching any content.** Non-negotiable; see section 7.

`.audit-baseline/` currently holds only `english-ids.json`. French needs
its own, produced by the generalised tooling from section 9.

### 6.2 What is already guarded

`src/data/curriculum-consistency.test.ts` **already covers all three
courses** and is CI-enforced, so these are guarded and currently passing
for French. Confirm, do not rebuild:

- duplicate unit / lesson / question ids
- out-of-range multiple-choice answer indices
- duplicate multiple-choice choice text, compared case-insensitively
  (this was a real generator bug, fixed 2026-09-22)
- empty prompt / choice / explanation strings
- orphaned `imageKey` references
- fill answers missing from their own word bank
- reorder answers that do not reconstruct from their token pool
- ids that would fail the server-side item-key regex

### 6.3 What is not covered — the actual work

1. **Malformed pack lines.** Every line must split into exactly two
   fields on `|`. A stray extra `|` silently truncates content.
   `packLineStats()` in `src/lib/english-id-parity.ts` does this for
   English; generalise it rather than rewriting it.
2. **Pack line-count consistency.** The generator expects 25 lines per
   pack. A short pack produces short lessons and nothing fails.
3. **Duplicate prompts across packs.** The same sentence in two packs
   yields two ids that a learner experiences as a repeat.
4. **Answer appearing in its own prompt.** English measured this and
   drove it from 12 to 5. French has never been measured, and has no
   ranking layer at all, so expect a worse ratio.
5. **Encoding integrity.** The French bank contains 18 distinct
   non-ASCII characters. Verify accents survive the pack → generator →
   JSON → iOS bundle path intact. A mojibake `é` is a content defect no
   existing test catches.

### 6.4 Phase 1 exit criteria

- A committed `.audit-baseline/french-ids.json`.
- An audit log at `docs/superpowers/french-content-audit-log.md`,
  modelled on the English one, recording **coverage** (what fraction was
  actually examined, per level) and **method** — not only findings. The
  English log's "How much 'No issues found' is worth" section explains
  why this matters; read it before writing yours.
- Every defect either fixed in place or explicitly deferred with a
  reason.
- `bun run test` green, including a new French id-parity test.

## 7. The id-stability rule — the one that can cause real harm

Question ids are **index-derived**: a question's id is a function of its
position within its pack.

- **Appending** a pack, or appending lines at the end, is safe. Existing
  ids do not move.
- **Inserting or removing a line inside a pack moves every id after it**
  and silently repoints real learners' saved review items. A learner's
  SM-2 history for one sentence starts pointing at a different sentence.
  Nothing fails. Nobody notices.

Therefore:

- Fix content by **replacing a line in place**, preserving line count
  and order.
- Take the baseline before editing; re-run the parity check after.
- If a re-baseline is genuinely required (appended content only),
  **diff it and confirm it is insertions only** before committing. The
  English work re-baselined twice (2,721 → 2,846 → 2,971) and verified
  this each time.

This rule is why "just regenerate the packs" is not an available
shortcut.

## 8. Distractor quality — blocked on one decision

This is the axis that made the English work worth doing, and it is where
French needs a decision rather than effort.

### 8.1 The French failure mode is different

Take a real French cloze pack:

```
Je ___ (parler) français.|parle
Je ___ (manger) une pomme.|mange
Je ___ (avoir) un chat.|ai
```

Distractors are pooled from the pack, so for `parle` the learner is
offered `mange`, `ai`, `suis`. Those are all verbs — so a pack like this
does **not** obviously exhibit English's word-class bug, because the
pack is already grammatically themed.

But they are the wrong distractors pedagogically. A question testing the
`je` form of _parler_ should offer `parles`, `parlons`, `parlez` —
**other forms of the same verb**. Offering a different verb entirely
tests vocabulary recognition rather than conjugation, and is trivially
easy.

**This is a French-specific finding, and English's fix does not address
it.** English ranking makes distractors _more plausible by word class_;
French needs distractors that are _morphological variants of the correct
answer_. That is a generator capability this repo has for no language.

Measure before designing: sample the 42 cloze packs and classify each as
single-verb (needs conjugation distractors), mixed-verb, or mixed-class.
The right fix depends on that distribution and nobody has looked.

### 8.2 Pair packs are the larger share

58 of 100 French packs are `pair`. Their failure mode is semantic
outliers inside a themed pool — "flowers in a time pack", in the English
log's phrase — which curation fixes per pack and which no ranking layer
can fix. Expect this, not word class, to be the bulk of French's
distractor work.

### 8.3 The blocking decision

If a POS or morphology layer is wanted for French, it needs a source.
`compromise` is English-only. This is the **same blocker** as
`docs/BACKLOG.md` §0.6.2 (extending generative content to French and
Spanish) — the two items should share one answer rather than each
picking independently.

Constraints that should drive the choice, learned the hard way on
English:

- The validator must be **the sole authority**. The English pilot's
  review found an LLM proposing tags unchecked produced confidently
  wrong output — 44.8% of the bank tagged "Verb", including `tax`,
  `card`, `discount`, `balance` and `refund`.
- Tags must be read from **the answer's own sentence**, not the bare
  word. The first English tag map scored 62% accuracy doing the latter;
  the contextual version scores 88%.
- Whatever is chosen must be **spike-verified against a known-correct
  conjugation table** before being trusted. The English pilot verified
  only 7 verbs this way and its own review flags that as insufficient.
- A map-relative metric cannot validate the map. English's POS ratio is
  a smoke test only — it sorts by the same map it measures, so it can
  detect the layer being removed but never a wrong tag. Independent
  evidence needs a hand-labelled sample, as in `answer-pos.test.ts`.

**Do not start 8.3 and phase 1 in the same session.** Phase 1 is
mechanical and self-contained; 8.3 is a research decision affecting two
languages and two backlog items.

## 8.5 Phase 1.5 — evidence gathering (DONE 2026-09-24)

Added after phase 1, to stop the blocked decisions in 8.3 from being
made on opinion. The rule was **recommend, do not implement**: no
ranking layer, no `bank-engine.ts` change, no new dependency. That was
honoured.

Full evidence: `docs/superpowers/french-distractor-quality-evidence.md`.

### 8.5.1 Cloze pack classification — all 42 read, not sampled

| Category              | Packs | Share |
| --------------------- | ----- | ----- |
| Single-verb           | 1     | 2%    |
| Mixed-verb            | 18    | 43%   |
| Single-class non-verb | 8     | 19%   |
| Mixed-class           | 15    | 36%   |

This settles the question §8.1 raised and could not answer:

- **45% (19 packs) are verb-conjugation drills.** Their distractors are
  already all verbs, so a word-class ranking layer has nothing to filter
  — confirming §8.1's hypothesis. These need _generated_ distractors
  (other forms of the line's own verb), not classification.
- **36% (15 packs) are genuinely mixed-class** — the case English's
  ranking layer exists for, and the only share of French where porting
  that approach would help.
- **19% (8 packs) are small closed classes** (pronouns, prepositions)
  carrying a structural collocation risk that no ranking layer can fix.
  `frb2p17` is the concrete case, with 17 self-references.

### 8.5.2 Self-reference rate — measured, and worse than first reported

Two distinct metrics were conflated at first and must stay separate:

- **Answer-leak** (the answer visible in its own prompt): **59 → 4**,
  fixed by phase 1 content edits. No English analogue; not
  ranking-layer territory.
- **Prompt-echo** (a distractor echoing a prompt word): **25 → 24**,
  essentially untouched, because nothing in phase 1 added a ranking
  layer. 24 is the standing ceiling such a layer would have to address.

**Correction to the first reading of this number.** It was reported as
"~1.0% of questions, close to English's pre-fix ~0.9%". Those figures
use different denominators. Self-referential distractors can only occur
on multiple-choice questions — a `fill` question has no distractors —
and English's 12 was measured across its 1,387 MC questions.

| Measure                           | Value     |
| --------------------------------- | --------- |
| 24 ÷ all French questions (2,500) | 0.96%     |
| 24 ÷ French MC only (1,303)       | **1.84%** |
| English pre-fix: 12 ÷ 1,387 MC    | 0.87%     |

Like for like, **French is roughly twice English's pre-fix rate**, not
comparable to it. Use the MC denominator for any future comparison.

### 8.5.3 Morphology spike — recommendation

**Recommended: `french-verbs` + `french-verbs-lefff`** (Apache-2.0,
actively maintained).

| Check                                           | Result                                                      |
| ----------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| 20 verbs × 6 persons, present                   | 119/120 (the one miss is a valid formal-register alternate) |
| Imparfait spot-check                            | 30/30                                                       |
| Coverage of the bank's 117 distinct infinitives | 100%                                                        |
| Unknown verb                                    | throws rather than guessing                                 |

Rejected, with reasons worth keeping: **`conjugation-fr`** is GPL-2.0,
which is a licensing problem for this private commercial codebase, and
**`nlp-js-tools-french`** has been unmaintained since 2017.

Failing loudly on an unknown verb is the right property and directly
answers §8.3's "the validator must be the sole authority".

**The architectural finding is the valuable part:** French's fix should
be **generative** — produce a line's distractors from its own hinted
verb — rather than **tagging**, as English does. That sidesteps
English's exact failure mode, where a tagger confidently mislabelled
44.8% of the bank as "Verb" and the map-relative metric could not see
it. A generator asked for "the other present-tense forms of _parler_"
either returns them or throws; there is no silent wrong answer.

### 8.5.4 Open gap in the spike — verify before adopting

The spike covers **present** and **imparfait**. The French bank's own
pack notes exercise more than that: Present (5 packs), past (5),
**subjunctive (2)**, **futur (2)**, **conditional (2)**, **passé
composé (1)**.

Subjunctive, conditional, futur and passé composé are **unverified**,
and they are both the tenses a conjugator is most likely to get wrong
and the ones the harder B2/C1 packs use. A real unverified line from the
bank:

```
Il est important que nous ___ (prendre) une décision.|prenions
```

That is present subjunctive of _prendre_. 100% coverage of 117
infinitives is **coverage, not correctness** — it says the library knows
the verbs, not that it conjugates them correctly in these moods.

**Before adopting: extend the spike to every mood and tense the bank
actually uses, against a known-correct reference table.** The English
pilot verified 7 verbs and its own review flagged that as insufficient;
20 verbs in one tense is better, but it is not yet evidence for the
tenses that carry the risk.

## 9. Tooling — generalise, do not fork

The audit tooling is English-shaped by name and by type:

| File                                | Problem                              |
| ----------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| `src/lib/english-content-dump.ts`   | exports `EnglishDump`; English-typed |
| `src/lib/english-id-parity.ts`      | `collectEnglishIds()`                |
| `scripts/snapshot-english-ids.ts`   | writes `english-ids.json`            |
| `scripts/audit-scan.ts`             | reads `english-<level>.json`         |
| `scripts/dump-english-questions.ts` | English-only                         |

**Add a course parameter; do not create `french-content-dump.ts`.**
Spanish is explicitly next and needs the identical generalisation —
forking now means doing it twice and maintaining three copies forever.
Keep `english-ids.json` as English's filename so the existing committed
baseline stays valid.

## 11. Explicit non-goals

- **Linguistic correctness.** Not an agent task; stays on the backlog
  until a native speaker is commissioned.
- **Adding listening or speaking to French.** Separate work, and it
  should follow a decision about whether French is meant to reach parity
  with English at all (§12).
- **Regenerating the packs.** Forbidden by section 7.
- **Consolidating English onto the shared engine.** A real backlog item,
  but not a prerequisite here — see §2.2.
- **Spanish.** Deliberately sequenced after French, one language at a
  time, at the account owner's direction.

## 12. Open questions for the account owner

1. **Is French meant to reach parity with English?** English now has
   listening and speaking; French has neither. The answer decides
   whether this audit is "harden what exists" or "step one of catching
   up". **Answered 2026-09-24: yes, French is meant to reach parity.**
   This unblocks planning for listening/speaking and further pack
   expansion, but does not itself start that work — it's a separate,
   gated initiative. See
   `docs/superpowers/french-distractor-quality-evidence.md` section 0.
2. **Will a native-speaker review be commissioned?** If not, the
   mechanical axes are the ceiling, and the documentation should say so
   plainly rather than implying French content has been verified.
3. **Is the placement exam in scope?** `placement-fr.ts` is a separate
   45-question pool, untouched by any of this work, and it assesses
   neither listening nor speaking in any language — so learners are
   placed by a text-only exam and then meet content it never tested.
